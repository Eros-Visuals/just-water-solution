02

Softeners, iron and sulfur treatment

Hardness leaves crust on faucets and glass. Iron leaves orange stains. Sulfur makes the house smell like eggs. Each one has its own fix, and many wells need more than one.

  • Softeners for scale, spots and dry skin
  • Iron filters for orange and rust staining

03

Aeration tank system

An aeration tank mixes air into your well water so iron and sulfur oxidize and drop out before the filter. It takes care of the egg smell and the orange staining without adding chemicals to your water.

  • Clears the sulfur egg smell
  • Takes out iron before it stains
  • No chemicals added to your water

How it works

Inside a whole-house RO system

It looks like a lot of equipment, but each piece does 1 job. Tap a number to find it in the photo.

  1. 1

    Sediment filter

    A large cartridge filter catches sand, silt and grit before the water reaches anything else.

  2. 2

    Carbon unit

    A carbon tank takes out chlorine, odor and bad taste, and protects the membranes that come next.

  3. 3

    Reverse osmosis membranes

    Water is pushed through fine membranes that hold back salt, dissolved solids and other contaminants.

  4. 4

    Storage tank

    Treated water collects in a tank of 250 to 1,000 gallons, so a reserve is always ready for showers, laundry and the kitchen.

  5. 5

    Pressure tank

    The pressure tank keeps treated water ready at steady pressure, so the pump starts and stops less often.

  6. 6

    Calcite unit

    Calcite brings the pH of the treated water back up, so it doesn't eat at your pipes and fixtures.

  7. 7

    Booster pump Optional

    A booster pump sends the treated water from the storage tank into the house at normal pressure.

  8. 8

    Ozone generator

    Ozone keeps the water in the storage tank fresh, breaking down bacteria, odor and sulfur.

  9. 9

    Smart water pump pressure controller Optional

    Runs the pump smartly, holding the pressure steady and protecting the pump from running dry.

  10. 10

    UV water purification Optional

    Ultraviolet light disinfects the water, neutralizing bacteria and viruses without any chemicals.

FAQ

Good to know

Have a question that is not here? Call or text (239) 821-4786.

Do I need whole-house reverse osmosis or just a softener?
It depends on your water. A softener handles hardness. If your well water is salty or high in dissolved solids, a softener will not fix the taste, and reverse osmosis is usually the answer. A water test tells us which one your home needs.
Why does a whole-house system need a storage tank?
Reverse osmosis makes water slowly and steadily. The storage tank keeps a reserve of treated water ready, and a pump with a pressure tank delivers it to the house at normal pressure, so you can run showers, laundry and the dishwasher at the same time.
Does reverse osmosis waste water?
Every reverse osmosis system sends part of the water it treats to a drain line, carrying away what the membranes removed. We size and set up each system for the home so it runs efficiently.
How often does the system need service?
It depends on your water and how much your household uses. Sediment cartridges are changed most often, and membranes and filter media last much longer. We set a service schedule for your system after the install.
